Variability and distributions

Every measurement in sport varies: between athletes, and within one athlete from day to day. The shape of that variation decides which summary describes it best. Pick a dataset, then add an unusual athlete and watch which summaries move.

What to notice

  1. For jump height the mean and median are almost equal: the data are roughly symmetric.
  2. Reaction times have a long right tail (a few slow responses), so the mean is pulled above the median. The median and IQR describe a typical athlete better.
  3. Add one very slow reaction time. The mean and SD jump; the median and IQR barely move. They are resistant to extreme values.
  4. Goals per match can only be 0, 1, 2…: a discrete count. A mean of 0.6 goals is still useful, even though no match has 0.6 goals.

How it works

mean sum of values / n SD √( Σ (value − mean)² / (n − 1) ) median middle value when sorted IQR 75th percentile − 25th percentile

For roughly normal data, about two-thirds of athletes lie within one SD of the mean. That rule does not hold for skewed data, which is why the plot shows both summaries.
