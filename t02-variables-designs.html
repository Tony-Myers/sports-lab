What to notice

  1. Ratio variables (time, distance, mass, counts) have a true zero, so “twice as far” makes sense. Interval variables such as temperature in °C do not.
  2. Ordinal scales such as RPE are ordered, but the gaps between points are not guaranteed equal. Many researchers still average them; if you do, say so and check that conclusions do not depend on it.
  3. Randomisation supports causal inference by breaking systematic links between treatment and confounders. Adherence, missing outcomes, interference and measurement still matter. Observational causal inference needs additional identification assumptions (Topic 3).
  4. Crossover designs need far fewer athletes when the effect wears off between conditions, but they bring order effects (Topic 4).

How it works

nominal categories with no order playing position ordinal ordered categories, unequal gaps race finishing position, RPE interval equal gaps, no true zero temperature in °C ratio equal gaps and a true zero sprint time, jump height, counts

Match the design to the question. Randomised trials support intervention comparisons; cohorts establish time order; case-control studies efficiently study rare outcomes; cross-sectional studies estimate prevalence. These are not a universal ranking of study quality.
